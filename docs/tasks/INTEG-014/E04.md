# E04 — Controlled hardware baseline

## Executive summary

The baseline confirms a substantial, repeatable transport slowdown; **E04
measured it, but did not implement a fix**. Uploading 65,535 bytes took
**590ms through the mainboard path versus 2,101ms through Extender**: 3.56 times
as long. Both paths used the same unchanged EMOS build, without rendering load.

Wire captures show the eZ80 sender sitting idle for about **73% of the forward
transfer despite P4 permission to send**. In the reverse direction, most idle
time comes from the Agon withholding permission. This supports investigating
EMOS execution overhead first; it does not isolate individual functions or
establish keyboard forwarding as the cause.

All **756 transfer cases and three wire captures passed**. Original firmware
was restored, and keyboard and SD checks passed. E05 will simplify the transmit
path in C, with correctness preserved and a frozen improvement threshold of the
greater of 5% or twice the observed timing range. **E05 awaits Author approval.**

## Forward timing

Medians of six pseudorandom-pattern trials per cell across two full invocations.
All four patterns passed; pattern3 is reported here because it is also the fixed
wire oracle. Rows descend by payload size. Endpoint time includes the existing
probe's admission-to-close interval around the normal upload; exact content
verification occurs afterward. Do not substitute these times for primitive
rendering cost. Percentage is `(Extender / mainboard - 1) × 100%` elapsed time.

| Payload bytes | Mainboard ms | Extender ms | Extra elapsed time | Mainboard payload bytes/s | Extender payload bytes/s |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 65,535 | 589.602 | 2,101.076 | +256.35% | 111,151 | 31,191 |
| 32,768 | 295.240 | 1,052.031 | +256.33% | 110,988 | 31,147 |
| 4,096 | 37.328 | 134.034 | +259.08% | 109,731 | 30,559 |

The nominal 8N1 ceiling is 115,200 wire bytes/s. Payload rates include the
measured interval's framing/control costs and are not raw UART bit rates.
The 65,535-byte Extender cell ranged only **2,101.025–2,101.148 ms**;
mainboard ranged **589.514–589.765 ms**. The gap is not run-to-run noise.

## Reverse and mixed timing

The return case is 256 records containing 2,048 useful bytes, plus the terminal
record: 4,626 UART bytes. MOS-clock end-to-end timing has 16.667ms granularity
(two clock units per 60Hz VBlank), so its displayed medians are coarse.

| Same metric on both routes | Mainboard ms | Extender ms | Extra elapsed time |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Return burst, eZ80 request to terminal reply, MOS-clock median | 83.333 | 116.667 | +40.00% |
| Mixed 65,535-byte forward completion, endpoint median | 672.814 | 2,207.112 | +228.04% |

Return ranges were 83.333–100.000ms mainboard and 100.000–116.667ms
Extender. Do not present the +40% median difference as high-resolution evidence.
Mixed timing includes the outstanding reverse request and its scheduling;
it is not pure upload latency. Both mixed passes were exact (72 saved rows).

The observer's reverse `elapsed_us` measures the producer's reply loop:
84.963ms mainboard versus **0.593ms Extender**. These are **not equivalent
end-to-end delivery measurements**: stock sends synchronously while P4 queues
output for later FIFO delivery. A claim that Extender returns the data in
0.593ms would be false. Use the MOS completion observations and wire interval.

## What the P4 UART wires show

Medians of three fresh 24MHz, 30-second captures; 720 million samples each.
These wires cover eZ80↔P4, not UART0 to the mainboard VDP. Consequently there
is no fabricated mainboard wire column or wire-level cross-device percentage.

| P4 UART window | Full span ms | Byte transmission ms | Idle while receiver permits ms | Idle under receiver backpressure ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| eZ80 → P4, 65,535 payload bytes | 2,099.394 | 568.880 | **1,530.514** | **0.000** |
| P4 → eZ80, 4,626 framed bytes | 109.610 | 40.132 | 0.140 | **69.338** |

Each column is a separate median; tiny rounding discrepancies are expected.
Byte occupancy uses decoded frame starts and nominal 8N1 duration. Full RTS-high
time is a different metric from its overlap with inter-byte idle; the latter
is shown in the backpressure column.

Forward P4 permission stayed open throughout the measured payload. Approximately
73% of that span was sender idle despite permission. Reverse idle was almost
entirely associated with **Agon** withholding permission. This corroborates
E03's candidate order without proving the exact proportion attributable to any
one function, interrupt source or keyboard packet.

All three trace hashes differ and each was decoded independently. Forward span
was 2,099.394014–2,099.394056ms, a one-sample range; reverse was
109.603347–109.611514ms. These unusually repeatable bounded workloads are not
claims of nanosecond absolute accuracy or statistical confidence across arbitrary
applications. The observed clocks, finite samples and no-render workload bound
the result. The prior UART pass's forward/return windows were
2,099.394/109.609ms, consistent with these repeats.

## Frozen material-improvement threshold

Apply the precommitted rule to the **same scope, payload, pattern and firmware
pair**: at least `max(5% of baseline median, twice observed full range)` reduction,
with no correctness regression. This is an engineering acceptance floor, not
a confidence interval or a claim that smaller effects are impossible to detect.
Use six matched endpoint samples and three wire samples again for a candidate.

| Primary comparison against original EMOS | Baseline median ms | Minimum reduction ms | Candidate median must be at most ms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 65,535-byte forward endpoint interval | 2,101.076 | 105.054 (5%) | 1,996.022 |
| 65,535-byte forward P4 wire span | 2,099.394 | 104.970 (5%) | 1,994.424 |
| 4,626-byte reverse P4 wire span | 109.610 | 5.481 (5%) | 104.130 |

For these high-resolution metrics, 5% dominates observed variation. Improvement
of wire-byte occupancy is not a target: fixed baud and unchanged byte count
already fix that cost. Reduce avoidable idle/backpressure while retaining exact
traffic. Forward and reverse results are separate acceptance observations;
a transmit-only candidate need not claim a reverse improvement.

A claim based only on MOS-clock measurements must additionally exceed two frame
quanta, **33.333ms**, and its observed range rule. For example the reverse
Extender MOS median needs at least 33.333ms reduction under that coarse metric;
prefer the wire comparison for smaller improvements. The repeatability CSV
includes the clock-resolution floor. Zero-median controls have no useful
percentage threshold. No control interval is blindly subtracted from a payload.

Legacy mode3 MOS prompt. The standard spoken alert follows the completed commit.

E04 establishes a stable baseline, not a fix. E05 is the next supervised step:
one transmit-path change at a time, preserving the stock-shaped UART operation,
EMOS ownership/error/timeout semantics, byte correctness and recovery. C is the
first candidate; assembly is not selected by this baseline. No E05 work or
experimental publication starts without the Author's next approval.
